# Wunderwaffe 3037

2D factory-builder RTS. Requirements: [`specs/requirements`](specs/requirements/README.md) (workbooks for viewing: `specs/xlsx/`).

## Setup

- Godot 4.7.2 .NET (`C:\Users\Public\Godot\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_mono_win64`)
- .NET 8 SDK
- VS Code with C# Dev Kit and godot-tools (recommended in `.vscode/extensions.json`)
- Git LFS (`git lfs install` once per machine)

## Layout

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `src/Wunderwaffe.Sim` | Game rules and state. Pure C#, **no Godot references**, integer-only, fixed tick. |
| `tests/Wunderwaffe.Sim.Tests` | xUnit tests for the sim, run against the real `game/data` content. |
| `game/` | Godot project. Draws the sim and turns input into `Command`s. |

| `game/data/*.json` | Content: items, recipes, buildings, goals, terrain. |

## Playing

The game opens on the title screen (`scenes/title.tscn`): **New game** (seed and map size), **Continue** (newest save), **Load game**, **Settings** (fullscreen, V-Sync), **Quit**. A match starts on the world map; the HUD's objective panel (top right) walks through the demo loop in `goals.json`: mine, smelt, lift off your starting scout, land it in the victory circle of a tile with coal and hold it until the tile is yours, ship coal home through ports, make steel, build a unit of your own. **Esc** drops the picked building, then clears the unit selection, then opens the pause menu (save, load, settings, quit). **F1** lists the controls, **F2** shows fps and sim cost. There is no tech tree (TT-001): every building and recipe is available from the start.

**New game** sets the match (MP-005, saved with it): map, seed, map size (3, 5 or 7 nominal tiles a side), AI opponents with a head start each, capture time, victory share and resource richness. The map is generated from the seed or is a scenario (WM-017): a whole map plus what each seat starts with, from `game/data/scenarios/*.json` or saved from a match with **Save as scenario** in the Esc menu (to `user://scenarios`). A scenario fixes the seats; every seat no person takes is an AI, which carries on with whatever the scenario gave it. Its JSON is meant to be edited: terrain, tile map and each tile's cell layers (deposits, platforms, frames, cables) and its deposit patches (amounts left, RS-004) are run-length rows over a legend of content ids, and each seat lists its tiles, buildings (with core stock) and units. A head start (MP-008, `head_starts.json`) stamps extra prefabs beside an AI seat's start and adds items to its core: a second scout, a stockpile, or both. Each seat also picks a **faction** (MP-009, MP-010, `factions.json`), or Random (fixed by the seed, so every machine agrees); a scenario may fix it. A faction is a template over the one shared catalogue: the part of it its seats can build (the rest is hidden from their build menu and refused by placement, blueprints and factories), its own variants of shared buildings (a blueprint with the base builds the variant for them, and the base for everyone else), and rules. Capture to build: the covered buildings stay locked (greyed in the build menu) until the seat has owned one it didn't build, then stay unlocked for good. Buff: a percent added to one stat (machine speed, power draw, factory build speed) of its own buildings. Shipped, with placeholder values: Charter Company (default, everything), Nomad Clans (no lasers or shields; unit factory, large core and precision jig by capture only) and Foundry Guild (machines 20% faster, the heavier Bulwark in place of the wall). Content refuses a faction whose buildings cost items nothing in it makes: the gating follows the recipes back to extraction, so a high-tier entry needs its prerequisites. Blueprints are shared; the library marks those that don't work for your faction. AI seats (MP-002) play through the same commands and queries as a person, on one framework (MP-006, `Ai/`): twice a second each seat reads the match, marks every tile expand, hold, reinforce or attack on an influence map, lets its modules (economy, expansion, production, defence, attack) propose scored actions, and issues the best within a command budget. They know no content id (MP-007): what they build follows from the content, starting from the cheapest unit prefab's cost and the current goal and chained back through the recipes to deposits, with buildings found by role. They build the lines that chain needs, claim a neighbour holding a deposit it misses (coal, today; the first scout waits until its core holds enough for the line there) and ship it home for the machines that need it, build that unit prefab, expand, defend threatened tiles, and send squads at the nearest enemy core. The AI keeps no memory between thinks, so loading a save or taking over a seat changes nothing about its play. A match ends (MP-001) when one player owns the victory share of all tiles, or is the last with a core; the end screen lists every player's stats, and the match can be watched on.

**Multiplayer** (MP-003, 2-8 players over ENet, UDP port 30370 by default): one player hosts, the rest join by address. The lobby lists the players; the host edits the match settings and starts. Every machine runs the same sim in lockstep: commands go to the host, which bundles them per 100 ms turn three turns ahead and sends the bundles to everyone; AI seats run on the host. State hashes are compared every second and a mismatch is shown to all players. Nothing pauses online (OQ-32), cheats are off, and when a player leaves, the host's AI takes over their side (OQ-46).

The HUD's top-left panel sums your stock over every tile you own; on the world map an armies list selects and jumps to each unit (UI-001). The alert list under the objective (UI-003) shows attacks, starved machines, finished units, tiles taken and lost, and eliminations, with a toggle per group (kept in settings); clicking an alert jumps to its tile or unit.

**Settings** (title or Esc menu) also has interface size (75-150%), colours (normal, red-green safe, blue-yellow safe: player colours and good/bad signals change) and **Key bindings…**, which rebinds every action (click, then press the key; modifiers count) and resets to defaults (UI-005). All of it is kept in `user://settings.cfg`.

Programs can play too (NF-009): `dotnet run --project src/Wunderwaffe.Cli` reads one JSON request per line on stdin and answers on stdout (`JsonDriver`): `{"op":"new","setup":{"Width":5,"Height":5,"Seed":1}}`, `{"op":"query","name":"PlanLine","args":{...}}` (any `GameQuery` member; `{"op":"queries"}` lists them), `{"op":"command","command":{"type":"place",...}}` (the save file's command JSON) and `{"op":"step","ticks":60}`. The GUI, the AI and this driver share the one command and query API.

Saves are a `GameSetup` plus the command log (`SaveFile`), in `user://saves` (`%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Wunderwaffe 3037\saves`). Loading replays the log (`Replay`) behind a progress bar, about 1.5 s per hour of play for a mid-sized factory, and warns if the replay doesn't end at the saved state hash (a save from different rules or content).

A Windows build (NF-005) comes from the "Windows Desktop" preset in `game/export_presets.cfg`: `Godot --path game --headless --export-release "Windows Desktop" ../build/windows/Wunderwaffe3037.exe` (needs the Godot 4.7.2 .NET export templates; `build/` is not committed).

Debug flags, after `--`: `--stress` (add `--headless` to skip rendering) opens the NF-001 benchmark world (8 viewed tiles of 2,000 buildings plus 200 rate-mode factory tiles) and prints the sim cost per tick; F3 fills the viewed tile with 2,000 buildings and does the same in-game. `--drive [shot.png]` (needs a window) plays a scripted unit session through the real input path. `--tour out_dir` (needs a window) clicks through title, new game, world map, a wheel zoom through the cross-fade into the home tile, tile, a Tab zoom out and back, scout sent to the coal tile, pause, save, quit and continue, save as scenario and a scenario match with an AI head start, logs what happened and saves a screenshot per step; it deletes the saves and scenario it made. **F4** adds 1,000 of every item to your first core on the viewed tile (single player only). `--net-smoke host` and `--net-smoke join` (headless is fine) run a short online match between two local instances and print each one's tick, hash and desync state.

In game, the tile view and the world map are one zoom (WM-007): the mouse wheel pulls back from a tile until, around the zoom where a whole tile fits the screen height, it cross-fades into the world map, and zooming back in over a tile you have presence on opens it (the point under the cursor stays put). Only the tile under the screen centre counts as viewed and runs the full sim; on the world map none does (OQ-36). **Tab** animates the same zoom between the world map and the last tile you viewed; your unit selection and picked building survive it. The world map is a blurred picture of every tile's local map, refreshed every few switches (3 by default; **Settings** changes it). On the world map: click selects a tile, double-click or Enter zooms into one you have presence on, **C** (or **Send units**) sends the selected units to the selected tile's victory circle, and **G** lands them where they are. Every tile's victory circle is drawn in its owner's colour, with an arc per player claiming it; Contested tiles are outlined red. Drag with the left mouse button from one of your tiles onto an adjacent one of yours to export an item to it (`[` `]` pick the item, `-` `=` set a per-minute limit, `\` switches back to unlimited); drag with the right button to stop.

## Rules of the codebase

- The sim only changes through `Command`s (lockstep multiplayer, replays — NF-004).
- No `float`/`double` in sim state; no Godot types in `src/`.
- Economy and territory tuning not yet in JSON (frame cost, refunds, circle radius and capture time) is in `Balance.cs`.
- **Rate mode (FB-012..014).** Only tiles some player is looking at (the `view_tile` command, sent whenever the view changes, so it is lockstep input) or that are Contested run the full entity sim. Every other tile moves items at rates solved from its building and belt graph (OQ-14), per group of port-linked tiles, so a shortfall in one tile's imports scales down the tiles that depend on it. Machines run at their boosted speed and output and at their power network's steady-state share: generators supply as much as the fuel reaching them lets them burn, consumers draw for as long as their throughput keeps them busy, and batteries (which only smooth peaks) are left out. Its belts and machines freeze where they are and carry on when it is viewed again; nothing is lost or made twice. Over 5 minutes a stable tile's output is within 5% of the full sim (tested).
- **Abstract travel (UC-005, UC-009).** Only the tile you look at (and any Contested tile) is rendered. A unit flying off it turns into a world-map node the moment it crosses the border, without stopping; a unit ordered on any other tile is a node from the start. Nodes follow their terrain-legal route at cruise speed with no physics. A node whose route passes through the viewed tile flies across it physically and goes abstract again on leaving. A node that meets enemy units materialises (the tile turns Contested): aggressive and defensive units stop to fight and carry on with their order once no enemy is left on the tile, passive and hold-fire units keep their course. A node that has stopped stays exactly where it is until you open its tile. Travel time stays within `AbstractTravel.TolerancePercent` (15%) of the full sim.
- **Snapshots (NF-008).** Every 5 minutes of play the sim keeps a state snapshot; a save carries the latest one, and loading restores it and replays only the later commands, falling back to a full replay if the hash doesn't match.

## World

The world is one global grid of cells (`WorldMap`), generated in phases (`World/Gen`), all integer and seeded (WM-009):

1. **Terrain** (`TerrainGen`): domain-warped fractal noise for elevation, sunk toward the rim so land forms continents and islands; sea level and land height by rank, so every seed has 55% land and the same share of high ground; priority-flood drainage, which fills deep depressions as lakes and turns cells with a big enough catchment into rivers; moisture from noise and nearness to water. Each cell takes the first terrain in `terrain.json` whose elevation and moisture bands contain it.
2. **Tiles** (`Partition`): seeds thrown at random, no two closer than ~0.8 of a nominal tile on land and ~1.15 at sea, grow by cheapest path; climbing, coastlines and rivers cost extra, so borders follow ridges, coasts and rivers. Tiny regions merge into a neighbour. The result is RISK-like territories with 2-9 neighbours; ocean tiles are ordinary tiles, from coastal to all water. `GameSetup` width x height is the world size in nominal 256-cell tiles (`Simulation.DefaultTileSize`, OQ-35; 3 to 7 a side); the tile count is about that.
3. **I/O zones** (`Zones`): see Ports below.
4. **Deposits** (`TileGenerator`): patches on buildable ground, centred by each terrain's deposit weights, with the regional rule (RS-003) as before.
5. **Victory circles** (`Circles`, WM-014): one per tile, ocean tiles included, radius `Balance.CircleRadius` (32, 1/8 of a nominal tile). It goes where the most deposit cells fall inside it, among centres that keep it `Balance.CircleClearance` (8) cells off the tile's border (just wholly inside if that leaves no deposit in reach; a tile too small for the circle gets its deepest cell).

**Starts** (WM-005, OQ-22): one player gets the land tile nearest the middle with room for a core and at least two land neighbours. With more players (`GameSetup.Players`, no menu option yet), `Balance.StartAttempts` seeded attempts each pick a first start (the middle one, then random ones) and add the rest by farthest-point sampling over tile-graph steps; the kept set has the most starts with a land neighbour to carry their regional deposit (so coal is one claim away), then the most even starting deposits, then the widest spacing. A start gets no free core: it gets a landed **starting unit** (a scout: thruster, 2x2 core, thruster on a 4x2 frame) nearest its circle's centre, whose core holds the starting items (WM-005). The start is content, not code (FB-021): each seat gets the prefabs `GameSetup.StartPrefabs` names for it (default `scout`, from `game/data/prefabs/scout.json`, whose `items` are the starting 600 copper ore and 300 iron ore), the first in the circle and any extras beside it; extra prefabs for an AI seat are its head start (OQ-47). Every building records the player who placed it (`PlacedBuilding.Owner`).

Two tiles are neighbours if they share at least 3 cell edges. A tile's local map is its bounding box in the world map: local (x, y) is world (OriginX + x, OriginY + y). Cells of the box that belong to other tiles are outside the tile; the tile view shows them dimmed. Water and peaks are unbuildable (`PlaceResult.BadTerrain`), except that a **floating platform** (FB-017; a building of kind `platform`, `onWater` in buildings.json, 2 iron ore) can be laid on water terrain. A platform is a floor layer, like frame: buildings, frames and landings can then stand on that cell. Removing on a cell with only a platform takes the platform up and refunds it. Tests and debugging can still hand-build square tiles with `AddTile(x, y, size)`.

A 5x5 world generates in about 0.3 s, 15x15 in about 2 s.

## Territory

- **Presence** (WM-015): you may enter, lift off and land on a tile you own or where you have a building or unit (`Territory.HasPresence`); with none, placement says `NoPresence`. Nobody builds or lands with any cell inside an enemy-owned victory circle while a building not of theirs stands in it (`EnemyCircle`). You can only remove your own buildings.
- **Build radius** (WM-015, OQ-58): every building operation (placing a building, frame or blueprint block, removing a building, taking up frame or cable, salvaging a wreck) needs every cell inside one of your build radii on that tile, else `OutOfRange`. Major buildings give one from the moment they are placed (`buildRadius` in buildings.json, measured straight-line from the footprint's nearest cell: core 24, large core 32, unit factory 12, turrets and shield generator 8); minor ones (conveyors, drills, cables...) give none. A flying unit gives 6 + 2 per live block (at most 32, `Balance.Vehicle*`) from its centre, so lost blocks shrink it; a landed unit's core is a building again. Radii never reach past their own tile. To build further out, place a core at the edge of your reach. A unit factory builds inside its own radius regardless. While a building or blueprint is picked, your radii are outlined on the tile.
- **Cores and paying** (WM-016, FB-019, OQ-37..39): there is one core kind with variants (Core 2x2, Large Core 3x3), each with a cost, HP, power and a per-item capacity. Each core holds its own stock; belts deliver into it up to its capacity, and a full core refuses so belts back up. What you can spend on a tile is what your cores standing on it hold: buildings, frames and blueprints there are paid from them in core id order, all or nothing (`Funds`); other tiles' stock and cores on units never pay. Refunds and items from removed buildings go to your core on that tile nearest the building, or are lost if you have none there; a destroyed core's items are lost. A core on frame lifts off with its framed group as a unit, taking its stock as cargo; landing puts the cargo back into the core. So expanding means landing a unit whose core brings funds. You are eliminated when your last core goes, whether it stood on a tile or rode on a unit; your last core can't be removed.
- **Claiming** (WM-006, CB-003): every tick, each player with a building (any cell) in a tile's circle who doesn't own the tile counts up; with none there their count drops to zero. The first to reach the capture time (`Balance.CaptureSeconds`, 30 s) takes the tile (lowest id on a tie) and every other player's building in the circle is destroyed; buildings elsewhere keep their owner. Capturing an enemy tile is the same rule, once their buildings in the circle are gone. The usual first foothold is a unit: fly it into the circle (C on the world map) and land it (G).
- **Debug destroy:** `DebugDestroyCommand` destroys a building as combat would (wreck left, held items lost).
- **Contested** (WM-004): a tile with a unit of someone other than its owner on it (an unowned tile: units of two or more players), `Territory.IsContested`.

## Tile entities

- **Belts** carry up to 4 items per cell at 4/128 of a cell per tick, spaced at least 32 apart, so a belt moves 7.5 items a second. Rotation 0 faces east, then clockwise. A belt hands its front item to whatever it faces, and takes items from behind and the sides, never from the cell it faces.
- **Machines** (drills, smelters, mills) buffer two batches of each input, run their recipe, and dump output into any adjacent building that accepts it, taking turns. A core accepts every item until it holds its capacity of that item. `MachineState.Status` says why one isn't running (starved or output full); the tile view marks machines stopped for 1.5 s with an amber or red dot (FB-006).
- **Ports** are 1x1 buildings placed in an **I/O zone**. Each tile has one zone per neighbour: the cells within `PortRules.ZoneDepth` (4) steps of the shared border, on the side facing that neighbour. A breadth-first search from all border cells gives each cell to the neighbour that reaches it first (lower id on ties), so zones never overlap. A port's zone, not its rotation, decides the tile it links to. Tile A's zone for B is paired with B's zone for A: an export port in one sends to the import ports in the other, taking turns, and items flow both ways, after `PortRules.TransitTicksPerTile` ticks. An export refuses items when the partner isn't yours, has no import port in the paired zone, or the link is full, so belts back up rather than losing items. Port throughput is set by the belts feeding them. With a port picked, the tile view shades every zone in its own colour and labels the tile it links to. The zone pair is the link a future rate mode (FB-012) would aggregate on.
- Removing a building returns the items it holds, with its refund, to your nearest core on the tile.
- **Power** (FB-005): a building's `power` is its draw, `powerSupply` what it makes. Buildings with a part in power (draw, supply, battery, node) join every such building they touch into a network (`PowerNet`). A core supplies 20, enough for a first drill, smelter and fuel press, so a landed scout powers an outpost on a claimed tile; a fuel generator supplies 40 while burning fuel (pressed from copper ore or coal). **Cables** (FB-020) lie on their own layer under buildings: lay them under or past anything, and they join every power building standing on or beside the run. A **Power Node** joins every generator, battery and node within 8 cells (not consumers); its reach shows while placing it. A linked export/import port pair carries power too, so one tile's generators can run the next tile's machines; both tiles' power panels show the shared network, and rate mode uses its joined balance. Right-click removes the building on a cell first, then the cable under it. Batteries store a surplus and cover a shortfall, at most `discharge` a tick each. A network short of power runs every consumer at the share it can meet; machines draw only while working. The side panel lists each network's balance (UI-002). On a unit, a block's onboard supply is its `powerSupply` plus `discharge`.
- **Boosters** (FB-016, the OQ-25 set as written): a booster improves every touching host whose `category` it lists, per `buildings.json` (`effects`, `maxPerHost`, `maxHosts`, `chain`, `halfWithout`). Gantry Rails count only in a straight line running outward from the host. Servo Banks give half without a Coolant Tower. Unit factories read their radius, block cap and build speed through `Boosters.Bonus`, build at their network's power share, and need a Precision Jig for blueprints with weapons or blocks costing a tier-3+ item (steel). Hovering a building lists its boosters and boosted stats.
- **Logistics** (FB-004): a junction passes items straight across (4 per lane); a router hands each item to the next neighbour that takes it, never back to its source; a sorter sends its filter item straight on and others left or right (**I** over a sorter steps its filter); a bridge sends items up to `range` cells to the next bridge facing the same way. **Storage** (FB-007): a container holds `storage` items and unloads only onto belts.
- **Statistics** (FB-011, IO-002, IO-005): each tile counts items made and used and items sent to each neighbour in 10 s buckets over 10 minutes. **O** opens the production panel (per-minute rates and a 10-minute graph); the side panel and the world map's **L** overlay show each link's items per minute and transit time.

## Units

A unit is a group of buildings standing on **frame** (key `0` in the build palette; a floor layer that overlaps buildings and costs 2 iron ore a cell), lifted off the tile by its **core** (every unit has exactly one). They are not defined in JSON.

- **Lift off:** `ActivateUnitCommand` on a core flood-fills the frame joined to it (up to 64 cells). Every building on those cells must lie wholly inside the frame, with no second core, no ports and no other player's buildings, or it is refused. It needs presence on the tile, not ownership, so a scout that landed on an unowned tile can lift off again. The buildings and frame leave the tile and become one unit; the core's stock and the items the buildings held become its cargo. In game, hover the core with no building picked and press **G**.
- **Body:** the unit's mass is the frame (2 a cell) plus each building's cost, so a heavier build is slower. Blocks are inert while the unit is active, and only add mass and thrust. Running machines inside a moving unit is planned.
- **Physics:** integer position, velocity, heading and spin (`Trig` is integer CORDIC trig). A **thruster** pushes the way it faces (rotation 0 faces east, as buildings do). Moving to a point, the unit turns its strongest engine toward the acceleration it needs, fires every engine in proportion to how well it lines up, and brakes into the target; turning rate is limited by its engines and its moment of inertia. Drag brings it to rest. Without a thruster it cannot move.
- **Collisions:** each cell is a circle. Units are pushed out of buildings (conveyors excepted) on any tile and out of each other, inelastically. Units path-find over the tile they are on and the next one (A* with clearance for the unit's size, straightened where there is a clear line, replanned when buildings change); with no way round they stop at the obstacle.
- **Locomotion (UD-004):** every unit is **ground**, **naval** or **air**, from its blocks: any **rotor** makes it air; otherwise a **hull** makes it naval (a hull with **wheels** as well can't be decided and is refused at lift-off); otherwise it is ground (wheels, or just thrusters). Ground crosses land, shallow water and rivers but not deep water or peaks; naval crosses deep and shallow water and rivers; air crosses anything. Terrain sets this through `ground`/`naval` in `terrain.json`. Pathing, border crossings and tile routes all obey it, and units are pushed back out of terrain they can't cross. A unit whose centre stands on such terrain (a boat built on land) can drive off it, and plans from the nearest cell within 8 it can use.
- **Tiles:** unit positions are world coordinates, so the tile a unit is on is just the tile of the cell under its centre, and crossing a border is no jump. A move finds a route of tiles through the terrain regions the unit's locomotion and size allow (`UnitNav`: per tile, the connected areas its centre can stand on, linked across borders), so a unit never needs to cross terrain it can't; a goal it can't reach moves to the nearest cell of the tile it can, and a tile it can't reach at all refuses the order. For each border the unit aims for a cell of the next tile, along the shared border and in the region its route enters, that makes the shortest trip, and plans its path over both tiles' buildings and terrain. Units can cross unowned and enemy tiles. At the edge of the world they stop at the border. Units collide with units on either side of a border.
- **Landing:** `DeployUnitCommand` puts the blocks and frame back on the tile, turned to the nearest quarter turn of the unit's heading, if the cells are free, buildable (ground or platform) and inside the tile, on any tile (the unit is presence there), but not in an enemy-held circle (UD-011).
- **Validation (UD-003):** lift-off also needs onboard power covering what the blocks draw (batteries, generators) and a way to move (a thruster today). Hovering a core on frame outlines the blocks that stop it.
- **Stats (UD-002):** cells, mass, HP, power draw/supply, cost, build time and DPS are derived from the blocks (`UnitStats`). A unit of more than 48 frame cells is a **Wunderwaffe** (WW-001): same rules, flagged in the UI.
- **Upkeep (UD-013):** a block may declare `upkeep` (items a minute); a unit pays the sum once a minute from its onboard storage (cargo) if that holds it all, else from the friendly resource radius it is in (FB-018). If it can't pay it is *unsupplied*: its engines stall (it drops move orders) until a payment succeeds; it retries every second.
- **Orders (UC-001):** move, **hold** (stop and stay; fires but doesn't chase), **patrol** (back and forth between where the unit was and the target), **retreat** (to the owned tile it can reach in the fewest crossings, without firing). Attack and attack-move are the same order type, run by combat (see Combat). A finished move leaves the unit anchored where it stopped.
- **Squads (UC-002, UC-003):** named groups (`CreateSquadCommand`) that share orders, formation and stance; ordering all of a squad's units moves them in the squad's formation. Formations: **box** (up to four columns, square to the map; loose units use it), **line**, **column**, **wedge** (point toward the goal) and **custom** (cell offsets forward/right), spaced by the units' sizes and turned to the direction of travel. Changing formation on the move re-forms the members still on the squad's order without breaking it. Squads are sim state.
- **Stances (UC-004):** **aggressive** (default: with no order it goes for the nearest enemy anywhere on its tile; on a move it stops to fight enemy units in range, then carries on), **defensive** (chases at most 12 cells from its anchor or from where it left its course, then goes back and carries on), **passive** (keeps course and speed, shoots only what is in range, never chases), **hold fire** (never fires or moves on its own). Orders are carried out whatever the stance. `UnitSim.StopsToFight` tells abstract travel which stances stop (aggressive, defensive). Combat asks `UnitSim.CanFire`.
- **Targeting modes (CB-007):** each unit and squad has one: **nearest** (default), **most blocks**, **most weapons**; its weapons pick among enemy units in range by it (ties: nearer, then lower id). An attack order's target comes first; a squad focuses on one target its mode picks; turrets always pick the nearest.
- **Rally (UC-006):** a factory (today, a core on frame) can have a rally point and a squad; a unit it finishes joins the squad and heads for the point. A core's setting is used up at lift-off.
- **Controls** (no building picked): left-click or drag a box to select your units (Shift adds), right-click to move them, and right-click past the tile's border to send them into the tile there. On the world map, right-click sends the selection to the clicked spot (refused with a message if no selected unit's locomotion can get there, such as a ground scout to an ocean tile's circle), and **G** lands it where it is. **H** hold, **P** then right-click patrol, **X** retreat, **Z** then right-click attack-move, **Q** make the selection a squad, **T** cycle its formation, **V** cycle stance (aggressive, defensive, passive, hold fire), **E** cycle targeting mode (nearest, most blocks, most weapons), **Ctrl+1-9** store a control group, **Alt+1-9** recall it, **Y** over a core then right-click to set its rally point (and squad, if the selection is one squad). With a building picked, left places and right removes, as before.

## Blueprints and unit factories

- **Library (FB-009):** `user://blueprints.json`, shared by every match. **B** opens it: rename, tag, duplicate, delete; blocks can't be edited (rebuild and record again). **K** over a core records the design on its frame (only if it would lift off, UD-001); **Capture area** records every whole building and frame cell in a dragged box, cores included.
- **Placing:** **Place** picks a blueprint as ghosts: **R** turns, **F** mirrors, left-click places all of it in one action and pays the total; nothing is placed if any block doesn't fit or the tile can't afford it. Extractors pick their recipe from the ore they land on; crafters keep theirs.
- **Unit factory (UD-006):** a 3x3 building with radius 6 and a block cap of 16 (boosters raise both, FB-016). Click it to select it, then **Build in factory** on a library entry and click where it should build, within its radius (distance from the factory's footprint, square). It refuses blueprints over its cap (frame cells for a unit, buildings otherwise) or outside its radius. Belts (or machines next to it) feed it the items the rest of the build still needs. It places one block at a time once that block is paid: frame, then systems, then armament (WW-004), each taking 0.1 s per item of cost. When the last block of a unit is placed it lifts the unit off and, in continuous mode, starts another. A building blueprint stays where it is built, so the factory shows *Blocked* until you remove it. Removing the factory returns its buffer.
- **Production setting (OQ-43):** the factory panel shows **Make manual** / **Make continuous**. Continuous (the default) starts the next copy as soon as one lifts off; manual builds one copy per **Build one** (projecting counts as the first) and takes no items while it waits. If a finished unit can't lift off (blocked, over the cap, no power), the factory **stops** with the reason on its panel and a factory alert, takes nothing more, and waits for **Resume**, which tries the lift-off again (OQ-44).
- **Prefabs (FB-021):** blueprint files in `game/data/prefabs/*.json` (`id`, `name`, `tags`, `blocks`, `frames`, optional `items` for the first core), validated at load (block ids, recipes, rotations 0..3, no overlaps, unique frame cells from (0, 0), unit blocks on the frame, items need a core); a bad one refuses to load. The library lists them after your own, marked PREFAB: place, build in factory, duplicate and copy code, but not rename, retag or delete. `GameQuery.Prefabs` lists them for the AI and other drivers.
- **Blueprint codes (UD-009):** **Copy code** on a library entry puts a `WW1:` text code on the clipboard (the prefab JSON, deflated, base64url); **Import code** adds the blueprint on the clipboard, refusing a damaged code or one with blocks this game lacks.
- **Megafactory (WW-002):** no separate building; a Wunderwaffe-sized blueprint needs a factory with enough boosters for its cap.

## Combat

- **Sight:** everyone sees everything (OQ-29). Weapons fire at any enemy (another player's unit or building) in range.
- **Weapons** are blocks (`"kind": "weapon"`): on a tile they are turrets, on a unit they are its guns. Each tick a weapon keeps its target while it is alive and in range, and every quarter second looks for the nearest enemy: unit blocks first, then buildings. Shells fly at the weapon's speed and hit the first enemy thing on their path; beams (speed 0) hit along their whole length the tick they fire. Your own blocks, bare frame and wrecks don't stop shots. Hits land on a block's exact square, so a unit is hit block by block.
- **Damage types (CB-002):** a weapon's `type` against a block's `resist` percent: damage = base x (100 - resist) / 100.
- **Per-block damage (UD-007):** a destroyed unit block stays attached as a dead wreck (the unit never splits) and stops working: lose the thrusters and the unit slows or stops, lose the guns and it can't fire. Destroying the core destroys the unit, and its blocks fall as wrecks where it was (on free buildable cells).
- **Wrecks (CB-006):** a destroyed building leaves a wreck in its cells; the items it held are lost. Units fly over wrecks, but nothing can be built on one. Within your build radius, pick any building and right-click a wreck to salvage 25% of its cost (paid where a removal refund would go).
- **Ammo (CB-004):** a gun with an `ammo` item fires one per shot from a magazine. Turrets are loaded by belts and machines next to them, and from a resource radius. Units reload from their cargo (ammo racks) and from a resource radius. A weapon with a target but no ammo stops and shows a red "!". Lasers use no ammo; on a tile they need power.
- **Resource radius (FB-018):** cores (16 cells) and supply depots (12 cells) share your funds on that tile (drawn from your first core there) with your units and turrets in range; a unit carrying a supply depot shares its cargo the same way. Resupply happens every half second. The radius shows round a selected unit that has one, and round the core or depot under the cursor.
- **Defences (FB-010):** gun and laser turrets, walls (high HP, resist kinetic) and shield generators. A shield absorbs enemy shots that cross into its bubble while it has charge, and recharges over time; shots fired from inside aren't stopped.
- **Repair (CB-005):** a repair bay heals your buildings and unit blocks within its radius, paying iron plate per 50 hp from its tile, and rebuilds a unit's destroyed blocks for their full cost. A repair block on a unit pays from its cargo, then from a resource radius.
- **Interdiction (IO-006):** a destroyed port's wreck disrupts the link: the world map draws a red "Disrupted" line until the wreck is cleared and a port rebuilt.
- **Orders:** with units selected, right-click an enemy unit or building to attack it: they close to weapon range and fire until it is gone. **Z** then right-click is an attack-move: they move there but stop to fight anything in range on the way. Attacks share the units' one order with moves, patrols and holds, so any other order replaces an attack. An attack on a unit also makes it the focus target of the attackers' squads: members fire at it first whenever it is in range. An explicit attack fires and chases whatever the stance. Territory's destruction (circle wipes) leaves wrecks too.

## Content notes

- `terrain.json` lists terrains in classification order: a generated cell gets the first whose `elevation` and `moisture` bands (0-1000, inclusive) contain it; sea level is 400. `buildable` gates placement and frames; `water` marks coasts and ocean; `river` marks the terrain used for river cells (buildable for now, so rivers don't cut tiles up); `deposits` weights where each ore's patches are centred. Content validation checks every elevation and moisture is covered.

- Item `tier`: 1 raw, 2 refined, 3 advanced, 4 exotic. A recipe can't output an item more than one tier above its best input (RS-001).
- Recipe `ticks` are at 60 ticks/second. A recipe with empty `inputs` is extraction.
- Item `deposit` (RS-004): deposits of the item are finite. Each patch (connected cells of one item, found when the tile is generated) holds `perCell` x its cells x the match richness percent, and every ore a drill makes comes out of the patch under it; an empty patch stops being a deposit and its drills show "mined out" (grey dot). `levels` are density thresholds in ore left per cell: the tile draws a patch with sprite `{item}_{level}` (0 = thinnest) when there is one, else the item's sprite fading as it thins. The hover panel shows what a patch has left. An item without `deposit` never runs out.
- Items with `"regional": true` (coal) never appear on a starting tile and appear on 35% of other tiles, always including at least one neighbour of each start (RS-003).
- Building `hp`: hit points of one placed block, kept while it rides on a unit. Must be > 0.
- Combat fields on buildings, all optional: `weapon` (`range` cells, `damage`, `cooldown` ticks, `type`, `speed` cells/s with 0 a beam, `ammo` item and `magazine`), `resist` (percent per damage type; each type must be dealt by some weapon), `shield` (`radius`, `capacity`, `regen` per second), `repair` (`radius`, `rate` hp/s, `cost` per `per` hp), `supplyRadius` (cells) and `storage` (cargo items on a unit). Kinds `weapon`, `shield` and `repair` require their block.
- Goal conditions: `produce` (an item, counted when a batch finishes, extraction included), `claim` (tiles owned), `ship` (items arriving through import ports), `launch` (units lifted off). Counters are per player, empire-wide (TT-005).
- `goals.json` is the guided objective chain, completed in order. `DemoLoopTests` plays the whole chain with real content and no cheats, so a content change that breaks the loop fails the tests.
- Building `upkeep` (optional): items a minute the block costs while on a unit. `radius` and `blockCap`: unit factories only, both > 0.
- The build menu has tabs (Production, Logistics, Power, Units, Combat, Boosters; each building's `menu` in `buildings.json`, **Shift+1-6** opens one). Within a tab, order (and hotkeys 1-9, 0) is `buildings.json` order, with cores and platforms last; crafters with several recipes get one slot per recipe, and the unit frame leads the Units tab.
